\section*{Question}

For both OTC and exchange, we all have this similar collateral posting business --- everyday, you will have to pay either counterparty the MTM $-$ existing collateral value. But why do we have a discount factor for OTC but no discount factor for exchange?

\section*{Answer}

\subsection*{Exchange-traded futures: discount factor cancels}

On an exchange, daily margin settlement is an \textbf{actual cash transfer that happens immediately}. If we hold one long futures contract from $t_k$ to $t_{k+1}$, we receive (or pay)
\[
F(t_{k+1}, T) - F(t_k, T)
\]
in cash \emph{today}, not at some future date. Since entering a futures contract costs zero, we have
\[
0 = \mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\!\Big[\,D(t_k, t_{k+1})\big(F(t_{k+1}, T) - F(t_k, T)\big)\;\Big|\;\mathcal{F}_{t_k}\Big].
\]
Because the interest rate over each settlement period is known at the start of that period, $D(t_k, t_{k+1})$ is $\mathcal{F}_{t_k}$-measurable and factors out:
\[
0 = D(t_k, t_{k+1})\Big(\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[F(t_{k+1}, T) \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_k}] - F(t_k, T)\Big).
\]
The discount factor \textbf{cancels}, giving
\[
F(t_k, T) = \mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[F(t_{k+1}, T) \mid \mathcal{F}_{t_k}].
\]
Applying this repeatedly with $F(T,T) = S(T)$:
\[
\boxed{F(t, T) = \mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[S(T) \mid \mathcal{F}_t].}
\]

The key intuition: the exchange converts one future payoff into a series of \emph{daily realized cash payments}. Each payment is settled immediately, so there is no time value to discount.

\subsection*{OTC forwards: discount factor remains}

In an OTC forward contract, the payoff $S(T) - K$ is a \textbf{single lump-sum payment at maturity} $T$. Even with daily collateral posting, the collateral is merely a security deposit --- it does not replace the future cash flow. The contract's value is still a claim on money to be received (or paid) at $T$, so it must be discounted:
\[
V(t) = \mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}\!\Big[\,D(t, T)\,\big(S(T) - K\big)\;\Big|\;\mathcal{F}_t\Big].
\]
Here $D(t,T)$ does \emph{not} factor out because it depends on the entire path of interest rates from $t$ to $T$, which is not known at time $t$.

\subsection*{Summary}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\hline
& \textbf{Exchange (Futures)} & \textbf{OTC (Forward)} \\
\hline
Cash flow timing & Daily (immediate) & At maturity $T$ \\
Collateral role & \emph{Is} the settlement & Security deposit \\
Discount factor & Cancels (known over one day) & Remains (random over $[t, T]$) \\
Price formula & $\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[S(T)]$ & $\mathbb{E}^{\mathbb{Q}}[D(t,T)\,(S(T)-K)]$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

In short: the exchange replaces one future payoff with a series of immediate realized payments (no discounting needed), while OTC keeps the payoff in the future and only posts collateral as insurance (discounting still needed).
